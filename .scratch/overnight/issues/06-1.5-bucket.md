**2026-10-15, integrator, from the session retro.** The cell name is validated three ways and all
three disagree, which is Q53 and Q64. Today `infra/bootstrap/variables.tf` accepts
`^[a-z][a-z0-9-]{1,19}$` (2 to 20, must start with a letter, may end with a hyphen),
`infra/modules/cell/variables.tf` accepts `^[a-z0-9][a-z0-9-]{0,20}[a-z0-9]$` (2 to 22, no edge
hyphen), and `contracts/infra-outputs.schema.json` takes the frozen appId shape. So `pc` applies
cleanly in both roots and produces a contract-invalid `outputs.json`, and `poc-` applies in
bootstrap and would name a bucket `spa-platform-poc-`.

A grilling found that, not a check, and it is the kind a check holds better than a reviewer: three
regexes in two languages agreeing is a mechanical property. **Add that check with the fix, in the
same change.** It belongs beside the two roots it compares, as a plan-time assertion or a small
script, and it asserts two things: the two roots' `cell` validations are the same expression, and a
probe set gets the same verdict from that expression as from the frozen schema's pattern -- with
`pc` and `poc-` in the probes, since those are the two that got through.

Not landed from the integrator's side, deliberately. The fix is in `infra/`, which is this
workstream's, and the check would fail on every push until the fix lands with it. A red guardrail
waiting on someone else's unmade decision is worse than no guardrail: the first person to meet it
reaches for `--no-verify`, which is how four unrelated checks got skipped twice in one evening.
